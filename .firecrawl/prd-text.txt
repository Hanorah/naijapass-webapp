1. Executive Summary 
 
Nigeria's 36 states and FCT present a complex, fragmented landscape for travellers, 
entrepreneurs, investors, and citizens. Accessing local laws, emergency services, reputable 
businesses, and government procurement opportunities across state lines requires navigating 
dozens of disconnected portals — if any exist at all. 
 
NaijaPass (formerly branded as OpenContracting on opencontracting.gritinai.com) is a unified, 
AI-powered civic and commerce intelligence platform that solves this problem. It serves as the 
definitive location-aware companion for anyone operating across Nigerian states — from the 
first-time traveller needing the closest pharmacy in Kano to a Lagos-based SME bidding on a 
government contract in Enugu. 
 
Core Value Proposition 
• One platform — all 36 states + FCT 
• Real-time AI chatbot for location-specific queries 
• Verified directory of local laws, businesses, and merchants 
• Government partnership layer for procurement, bidding, and civic feedback 
• Emergency, health, and utility information at your fingertips

3. Problem Statement 
 
3.1 The Fragmentation Problem 
Nigeria's federal structure creates significant information asymmetry between states. A traveller 
arriving in Imo State from Lagos has no single resource to quickly understand: 
• Which businesses and markets are open and verified in that locality 
• What the applicable state laws and regulations are for their activity 
• Who to call in a medical or security emergency 
• How to bid on or track a government project in that state 
 
3.2 Government Procurement Opacity 
Federal and state government procurement in Nigeria suffers from limited public visibility. Small 
businesses struggle to discover, bid on, and monitor contracts. Citizens cannot easily track how 
public funds are deployed or provide feedback on project quality. 
 
3.3 Opportunity 
With over 220 million Nigerians, rapidly growing smartphone penetration, and increasing 
government digitisation initiatives, NaijaPass is positioned to become the definitive civic-
commerce layer for the country.

5. User Personas 
 
5.1 The Interstate Traveller — 'Tunde' 
Tunde is a 34-year-old sales executive from Lagos who travels to 3–5 states per month for 
business. He needs quick local intelligence — nearest petrol station, hotel, hospital, local 
emergency numbers — without hunting across multiple apps. He values speed and accuracy 
above all else. 
 
5.2 The SME Owner — 'Amaka' 
Amaka runs a construction firm in Enugu and wants to expand to other states. She needs to 
understand procurement opportunities, local regulations for contractors, and how to meet 
compliance requirements in states she hasn't operated in before. Government contracts are a 
major growth lever for her. 
 
5.3 The Citizen Watchdog — 'Emeka' 
Emeka is a civil society activist in Kaduna who wants to track government projects in his 
community — road construction, school rehabilitation, water supply — and submit feedback or 
flag irregularities. He values transparency and accountability tools. 
 
5.4 The New Resident — 'Fatima' 
Fatima recently relocated from Abuja to Port Harcourt. She needs to understand her new state's 
laws, register a business, find trusted service providers, and know her local government 
contacts. She is moderately tech-savvy and prefers a mobile-first experience.

6. Features & Requirements 
 
6.1 State Intelligence Hub 
Overview 
A structured, searchable knowledge base for each of Nigeria's 36 states and the FCT. Each 
state page is a living document updated in real time via government data feeds and verified 
contributor networks. 
Key Features 
• State law library — searchable database of applicable laws, regulations, by-laws, and 
executive orders per state 
• Government directory — ministries, departments, agencies (MDAs), commissioners, 
LGA contacts, and office addresses 
• Emergency & utility numbers — police, fire, ambulance, NEPA/EKEDC, water board per 
LGA 
• Infrastructure status — road conditions, power supply reports, internet connectivity index 
• State business environment score — derived from regulatory burden, contract 
enforcement data, and user ratings 
Non-functional Requirements 
• Data updated at least every 24 hours via API integrations and editorial review 
• Offline-first: core state info cached locally for use without internet 
• Available in English with plans for Pidgin and major regional languages (Hausa, Yoruba, 
Igbo) 
 
6.2 AI-Powered Location Chatbot 
Overview 
A conversational AI assistant that understands the user's selected state/LGA and provides 
instant, contextual answers. This is the primary engagement surface for most users. 
Sample User Interactions 
• 'What is the emergency number for Surulere, Lagos?' → Instant verified answer 
• 'Find me the closest eatery open now near Kano Airport' → Map + list response 
• 'What permits do I need to operate a food business in Rivers State?' → Regulatory 
summary with source link 
• 'Is the Sagamu–Ore expressway currently passable?' → Crowd-sourced + official report 
• 'What government contracts are open for bid in Plateau State this month?' → Filtered 
procurement listings

• Built on a fine-tuned LLM with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) over the State

6.3 Verified Business & Merchant Directory 
Overview 
A curated, crowd-validated directory of businesses and merchants organised by state, LGA, and 
category. Combines official business registration data with user reviews. 
Key Features 
• Business listing with category, location, hours, ratings, and contact info 
• Verified badge system — CAC registration number, tax ID, or manual verification by 
NaijaPass team 
• User reviews and ratings with AI moderation to prevent fraud 
• Premium listings — businesses can pay for enhanced visibility, featured placement, and 
analytics 
• Integration with map services for turn-by-turn directions 
• 'Open now' and 'Accepts POS/transfer' filters 
Categories (Phase 1) 
• Hospitality: Hotels, guesthouses, restaurants, bars 
• Health: Hospitals, pharmacies, clinics, diagnostic centres 
• Transport: Car hire, logistics, fuel stations 
• Finance: Banks, microfinance, MFBs, forex bureaux 
• Professional: Legal, accounting, engineering, consulting 
• Retail: Markets, supermarkets, specialty stores

6.5 Emergency & Safety Layer 
Overview 
A dedicated safety feature accessible with one tap from any screen, regardless of the user's 
current context. 
• SOS button: sends user's GPS location to pre-configured emergency contacts 
• State-specific emergency numbers pre-loaded and callable directly 
• Crowd-sourced safety alerts: users can flag insecurity, road blockades, flooding 
• Hospital nearest-to-me with bed availability (via partnership with NHIA) 
• COVID/health outbreak notifications from NCDC 
 
6.6 User Profile & Location Preferences 
• Set home state and up to 3 'travel' states 
• Notification preferences: news, procurement alerts, emergency alerts per state 
• Business profile: for merchants registering their business on the platform 
• Civic profile: for citizens engaging with government feedback tools 
• KYC-lite: BVN/NIN optional verification for procurement bidding module

8.2 Data Architecture

beyond 30 days

• NDPR (Nigeria Data Protection Regulation) compliance mandatory at launch 
• ISO 27001-aligned security practices 
• Regular penetration testing, especially for procurement and civic feedback modules 
• End-to-end encryption for whistleblower submissions

• State Ministries of Finance / Procurement — State-level contract data and bid

USSD fallback 
• Nigerian Bar Association (NBA) — Validated state law content

12. Open Questions & Dependencies 
 
• Legal: What is the liability framework for incorrect legal information displayed on the 
platform? Requires legal counsel review. 
• Government: Which states should be prioritised for MOU outreach in Q1? Recommend 
Lagos, Abuja, Rivers, Kano, Enugu. 
• AI: Should the chatbot be branded separately (e.g., 'NaijaBot') or integrated as a 
seamless platform feature? 
• Monetisation: Is the transaction fee model viable given sensitivity around government 
procurement? Needs legal and ethical review. 
• Content: What is the editorial governance structure for state law updates? Who is 
accountable for accuracy? 
• Data: Can procurement data be legally aggregated and republished without government 
MOU? Requires legal opinion.
